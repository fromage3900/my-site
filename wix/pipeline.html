How Melodia gets made.

Melodia moves between Unreal Engine 5.8 and a handful of tools I use for specific jobs: Blender and Houdini for procedural work, Cascadeur for animation, Substance for materials, and TouchDesigner for audio-reactive visuals. This page is the practical version of that pipeline — what is connected, what has been tested, and what still belongs in the lab.

What moves where

A small pipeline with clear jobs.

I use different tools because they are good at different things. The useful part is the handoff: can I move an asset, animation, procedural result, or control signal into the next stage without losing the art direction?

Unreal Engine 5.8 · the world

Final environment assembly, gameplay context, materials, foliage, PCG, lighting, and presentation live here. Unreal captures remain the visual authority when a browser study or DCC preview disagrees.

Houdini / Hython · variation + fields

I use Houdini for procedural variation, cymatics-inspired textures and geometry, field-driven experiments, and repeatable asset generation. In-editor Houdini Engine cook/bake claims stay labeled experimental until I have a current machine-side capture.

Blender · fast procedural art

Geometry Nodes and conventional modeling are useful for fast form studies, ornaments, and laptop-side procedural work. The point is not generator count; it is whether the result survives contact with the actual scene.

Cascadeur · character motion

Video mocap, posing, cleanup, and retarget work sit here before animation returns to Unreal. I keep the portfolio focused on the resulting motion rather than pretending the transfer step is the artwork.

Substance · surface decisions

Painter and related texture tools handle authored surface work before materials are judged again under the lighting and shader response of the Unreal scene.

TouchDesigner · when the music leaks out

TouchDesigner is my experimental audio-reactive lane for concert visuals, OSC/Spout studies, and visual systems that respond to sound. Browser recreations are studies, not live Unreal telemetry.

Production boundaries

What I will and will not claim.

The pipeline page used to read like every experiment had already become infrastructure. This is the September version: the proven work stays visible, and the interesting unfinished stuff is allowed to remain unfinished.

Current project record

GitHub holds code, documentation, issues, review evidence, and the site itself. Large binaries use LFS where appropriate. Dated snapshots stay dated instead of quietly becoming “current.”

Procedural does not mean automatic

PCG, Houdini, and Geometry Nodes help me generate options and repeatable structure. Composition, selection, cleanup, lookdev, and final presentation remain art decisions.

Browser studies are not engine proof

The WebGL toys are useful because they make objects and ideas explorable. They do not prove Unreal renderer parity, packaged-game performance, or a production integration that has not been captured.

Experiments get a label

Old dashboards, model-tooling tests, and one-off automation experiments are preserved as noindex historical evidence. They are not part of the recruiter-facing story unless the current work genuinely depends on them.

Typical flow

From idea to something I can actually show.

The exact route changes per asset, but the production rhythm is deliberately boring: make, move, judge, revise, capture.

01
Author. Model, sculpt, simulate, paint, or generate the thing in the tool that makes the most sense.
02
Bring it into Unreal. Check scale, silhouette, material response, animation, and whether it belongs in the world at all.
03
Connect the system only when it earns its keep. PCG, audio response, procedural variation, or gameplay hooks exist to support the piece—not to decorate the README.
04
Capture evidence. Beauty first, then the smallest technical proof needed to explain how it works.
05
Publish selectively. Strong work moves into Selected Art or the relevant case study. Diagnostics and half-finished experiments stay in the lab.
